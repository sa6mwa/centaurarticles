Companies are buying new productive capabilities while preserving the
arrangements that prevent them from using those capabilities fully.
Torsten Slok's assessment of the missing corporate AI payoff and the
survey by Justine Hastings, Chad Syverson and Seth Zimmerman bring the
contradiction into focus: employees already using AI still spend a
substantial part of their working lives assembling information and
coordinating across functions. This essay connects their findings to the
Coordination Shift, the expansion of work that a person or small
human--AI unit can carry. It argues for reorganizing around complete
outcomes, moving information and decision authority together, and
retiring dependencies that no longer justify their cost. The urgency
extends to safety, where delayed authority can leave a known hazard
uncontrolled. Productive capability becomes useful when the institution
allows people to act on it.
